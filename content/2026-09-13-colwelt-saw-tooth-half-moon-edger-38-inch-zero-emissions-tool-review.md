Gas-powered lawn edgers burn fuel, emit noise pollution loud enough to disturb neighbors and wildlife, and require periodic maintenance involving oil changes and spark plugs. A handheld gas edger emits roughly 0.5–1 lb of CO2 per hour of use, plus unburned hydrocarbons and nitrogen oxides — the same toxic cocktail as car exhaust. ## Sustainable Angle

Replacing a gas-powered edger with a manual one eliminates an entire category of small-engine pollution. Gas edgers, like gas mowers and trimmers, run on two-stroke engines that can burn up to 30% of their fuel as unburned hydrocarbons. Over a typical season of 20–30 uses, that adds up to several pounds of toxic emissions — enough to offset the environmental benefit of even a small home garden. The manual edger also has a dramatically longer lifespan. Gas engines fail, batteries degrade, and electronics break. A well-maintained steel edger blade can work for 10+ years, and when it finally wears out, steel is recyclable.

The Colwelt edger also supports broader sustainable landscaping practices. Clean edges reduce turf creeped into garden beds, which means less competition between weeds and planted crops. Fewer border weeds means less need for herbicide application — keeping your garden beds truly organic.
