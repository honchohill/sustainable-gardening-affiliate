## Why This Matters for Sustainable Gardening

Traditional seed starting relies on two unsustainable practices: plastic pots that end up in landfills after a single use, and peat moss harvesting that destroys ancient carbon-sequestering bogs. The Back to the Roots plantable pots tackle both problems. They're made from coconut coir, hemp, and corn — all rapidly renewable materials that decompose naturally after transplanting. You literally plant the pot into the ground, eliminating transplant shock and waste in one motion. For anyone serious about reducing their garden's environmental footprint, seed starting is an easy and often overlooked place to start.

## Sustainable Angle

The coconut coir used in these pots is a byproduct of the coconut industry — a circular economy win that repurposes what would otherwise be agricultural waste. Hemp in the fiber provides a rapidly renewable, low-input crop that sequesters carbon during growth. Corn starch serves as a natural binder, replacing petroleum-based adhesives found in many "recyclable" paper pots. The pre-filled organic mix avoids peat entirely, protecting ancient peatland ecosystems whose destruction releases millions of tons of CO₂ annually. Even the cardboard outer packaging is compostable.
